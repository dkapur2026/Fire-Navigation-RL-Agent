## 1. The pitch

A house is on fire. A rescuer has to get the people inside out: a dog, a spouse, two small children, or some mix of
them. It plans its route from what it believes about the house, such as how fast carpet burns or which doors open.
Then, partway through the rescue, the world changes and nobody says so. A door jams on the way in. The carpet starts
burning three times faster. Or the rescuer hears a child the family never mentioned, hiding upstairs, and now has a
new job. Most planners keep trusting their original assumptions and fail. emberpath's agent notices that the world has
stopped behaving the way it expected, works out *which* assumption broke, fixes just that one, re-plans, and says in
plain English what it changed and why. That is the interesting problem: a plan is only as good as the beliefs behind
it, and real environments change those beliefs mid-mission. The agent has to learn the change from a handful of
observations, during a single rescue, without being told.

Learned agents were tried against it too. The planner adapts by detecting and repairing its beliefs mid-run; a
learned agent has no rulebook and instead learns, from thousands of practice fires in which the changes happen, a
policy that copes with them. Deep RL from scratch (PPO) fell far short of the planner. A network that first imitated
the adaptive planner and was then fine-tuned with PPO matched it and edged past it (pooled score 0.93 against 0.90 on
8 ranch settings, one training run), though it does worse on a combination of changes it never practised. A world-model
learner (DreamerV3) learned nothing on this budget and was abandoned (section 6).

## 2. How it works, without the jargon

**The setup.** The house is a grid of squares drawn as a floor plan: walls, doors, carpet, wood or tile floors, and
exits. Fire spreads from square to square at random, faster over carpet than tile, and starts slowly: it takes a turn
only every third tick at first, then every tick from tick 30 (docs/SPEC.md W18). The rescuer moves one square per
tick (one turn of the clock). It carries a **rulebook**: its best guess at how the world works ("carpet next to a
flame catches fire about 16% of the time", "this door opens"). Every move it makes comes with a sentence explaining
it ("Heading to Max (child), 13 steps away").

**Three rescuers, same fire.** Every scenario is run by three versions of the rescuer, facing exactly the same fire:

* the **baseline** keeps the textbook rulebook and never updates it;
* the **oracle** cheats: it is always handed the true, current rules. It shows the best this planner could do;
* the **adaptive** agent starts with the textbook rulebook, like the baseline, and has to work out what changed from
  what it sees.

The project's score asks how much of the distance from baseline to oracle the adaptive agent covers.

**One rescue, step by step.** On the wide `house` floor plan, a married household: the rescuer and their spouse. Watch
[`gifs/house/married_hidden_dependent_adaptive_seed0.gif`](../gifs/house/married_hidden_dependent_adaptive_seed0.gif)
next to the [baseline](../gifs/house/married_hidden_dependent_baseline_seed0.gif) and
[oracle](../gifs/house/married_hidden_dependent_oracle_seed0.gif) runs of the same fire. Each frame's caption is the
agent's own reason for that move. (The GIFs are generated by `make gifs`; the `gifs/` folder is not tracked by git, so
run that first if the links are missing.)

1. **The fire starts.** The rescuer begins two steps from an exit. Its rulebook says only the spouse is in the house,
   and the spouse goes for the exit on her own. So the plan is simple: "Route clear: heading to the exit, 2 steps",
   then wait there for her.
2. **Something changes.** A neighbour's child, Lily, is on a sleepover and hiding in a far bedroom. She is not on the
   household list. The agent can't see her. Its only clue is sound: it hears someone calling when it is within four
   squares, and from tick 10 onward someone outside shouts where she is, so the news always arrives eventually.
3. **The agent notices.** At tick 10 the calls start reaching it. Its rulebook said nobody else was in the house, and
   the evidence now says otherwise. That gap between expectation and evidence is what the agent watches for.
4. **It updates its beliefs and explains itself.** At tick 12 it says: *"Updating my beliefs: Someone not on the
   household list is hiding at (3, 29). I heard someone at (3, 29); going to find them, 30 steps away."* It goes back
   into the burning house, finds Lily and brings her out. Result: 2 of 2 rescued in 62 ticks.
5. **Compare.** The baseline never believes the calls. It waits at the exit until time runs out, and Lily is lost
   (1 of 2). The oracle knew about Lily from the first tick and got both out in 55 ticks.

A second example of a changed world: in
[`gifs/house/family_door_jam_adaptive_seed0.gif`](../gifs/house/family_door_jam_adaptive_seed0.gif) a door on the
rescuer's route jams. The agent bumps into it at tick 8, says *"Updating my beliefs: The door at (5, 4) is jammed shut"*,
takes the long way round to the child, and gets all three family members out. The
[baseline](../gifs/house/family_door_jam_baseline_seed0.gif) keeps trusting the door and loses one of them.

**Why the people differ.** The dog is fast but bolts from fire and follows unreliably. Children walk at half speed,
so the order you fetch them in matters, and a parent can carry one when flames get close. The spouse doesn't wait to
be rescued: she fetches the nearest child or heads for an exit, but panics near flames or at a jammed door. To the
simulator these are just different numbers. They make the stakes easy to picture and give the planner real choices.

**Why it explains itself.** A rescue system people can't question is one nobody will trust. Every move carries a reason,
and every change of belief is announced, along with the runner-up explanation when the agent isn't sure.

## 3. How it works, technically

### 3.1 Rules as coins: `Rules`, `RulePatch`, `Event` (`rules.py`)

Every rule of the world is a **Bernoulli probability** (the bias of a coin) addressed by a string key:

| key | meaning | default |
|---|---|---|
| `spread_prob:<material>` | chance fire jumps from a burning cell to a 4-neighbour of this material, per fire turn (every tick from tick 30; fewer before: W18) | wood 0.10, carpet 0.16, tile 0.15, door 0.07 |
| `follow_prob:<kind>` | chance a bonded dependent manages to follow, per allowed tick | dog 0.70, spouse 0.95, child 0.90 |
| `force_prob:door` | chance the rescuer breaks open a jammed door per try | 0.10 |
| `door_jammed:<r>,<c>` | 1.0 if that door cannot be opened | 0.0 |
| `occupied:<r>,<c>` | 1.0 if an *unexpected* person is at that cell | 0.0 |

`door_jammed` and `occupied` are the **boolean families**: near-deterministic coins. The world holds the TRUE `Rules`;
each agent holds BELIEVED `Rules`. A **novelty** is a `RulePatch(key, value)` applied to the truth at a scheduled tick.
A **repair** is a `RulePatch` applied to the belief. The agent has adapted correctly exactly when the patch it infers
matches the one the world applied. Every random trial the simulator makes on a rule is logged as
`Event(step, key, outcome)`, and that log, plus what the rescuer can observe, is the only evidence an agent gets.

Novelty presets (`scenarios.py`), each built per blueprint so the house stays solvable:

| preset | what changes | when |
|---|---|---|
| `door_jam` | a door on the rescuer's mission route jams (`door_jammed:<door>` = 1) | tick 1 |
| `carpet_flashover` | carpet spread 0.16 -> 0.45 | tick 2 |
| `compound` | `door_jam` at tick 1, then `carpet_flashover` at tick 10 | 1 and 10 |
| `hidden_dependent` | a visiting child hides at a spare blueprint slot (`occupied:<slot>` = 1) | tick 0 |

Since 2026-11-09 `door_jam` jams several doors drawn per seed (house 2-4, ranch 1-2, studio 1); see docs/SPEC.md W17 and docs/ARCHITECTURE.md.

`hidden_dependent` is the "new task mid-run" case: an extra person to rescue, expressed in the same rule grammar.

### 3.2 The detector: Beta-Bernoulli with Bayes factors (`novelty.py`)

For each rule key the detector keeps success and failure counts and computes a **log Bayes factor**: how much better
the data is explained by "the coin's bias is unknown" (a Beta prior, the standard prior for a coin's bias) than by
"the bias is what I believe" (a point null):

```
log BF = log P(data | p ~ Beta(a, a)) - log P(data | p = belief)
```

A key is flagged when the score passes `threshold` = 6.0. Stochastic keys use `Beta(1, 1)` and need `min_trials` = 5.
Boolean keys use a U-shaped `Beta(0.1, 0.1)` against a near-deterministic null (contrary-outcome likelihood 1e-4), so a
single contrary outcome (bumping a door that should open, hearing a voice where nobody should be) scores about 8.5 and
flags at once. Agreeing outcomes can only lower the score.

**Checking every tick is safe.** Normally, testing again and again inflates false alarms. A Bayes factor against a
point null with a fixed prior is a *mixture sequential probability ratio test* (SPRT): under the null it is a
non-negative martingale, so Ville's inequality bounds the chance it *ever* crosses the threshold by e^-6, about 0.25%
per key, however often it is checked. No growing threshold is needed. Each `Finding` reports the posterior mean, a 90%
interval (from a 1001-point grid), the trial count and the score. After a repair, `reset_belief` keeps the evidence and
moves the null to the new belief. Optional exponential forgetting (`decay`) exists but is off, because it breaks the
martingale guarantee.

### 3.3 Repair: a search over rule edits, with a runner-up (`repair.py`)

The detector says "these rules look off". The repairer answers "what is the smallest edit to my rulebook that explains
everything I've seen?":

* **Hypotheses.** Only flagged keys that have evidence. Probabilities: every value on a 0.05 grid plus the detector's
  posterior mean. Boolean keys: the toggle. Single edits first; pairs (up to `max_edits` = 2) only when the best single
  edit leaves a flagged key still surprising.
* **Score.** Total Bernoulli log-likelihood gain over all stored events, minus `complexity_penalty` = 2.0 nats per
  edit, so one strong explanation beats a pile of weak ones.
* **Confidence.** `runner_up_margin` is the score gap to the best hypothesis that edits a *different* set of keys.
  Below `ambiguity_margin` = 1.0 nat the result is `ambiguous`, and the agent says so: *"I think this changed: X. But
  Y would explain what I saw almost as well (margin 0.4); acting on my best guess."*

The **adaptive agent** (`agents.py`) wires these together: it runs the detector only when events arrive, the repairer
only on fresh findings, swaps the repaired rules into the planner, and puts the change at the front of that tick's
reason. A surprise that the repairer judges to be bad luck is reported once ("looks like bad luck; keeping my
beliefs"). A `naive_repair` ablation skips the search and moves flagged rules straight to their posterior mean.

### 3.4 The hazard-aware planner (`planner.py`)

A symbolic planner with no learning. It plans from the *believed* rules, so wrong beliefs give wrong plans. That is
how repairing a belief turns into a better decision. Each tick:

* **Fire arrival forecast (`fire_eta`).** Dijkstra (shortest-path search over weighted cells) from every burning cell,
  where entering a cell costs its expected crossing time, `1 / believed spread probability` ticks. The fire moves only
  between 4-neighbouring floor cells: it never passes through walls, into an exit, or through already burnt-out cells.
* **Pricing cells.** Cells the fire is forecast to reach before the rescuer cost extra
  (`1 + risk_weight x risk`). Burning cells and doors believed to be jammed are walls. A believed-jammed door seen from
  its hallway side costs `1 / force_prob` ticks instead, because it can be forced from there.
* **Save margin.** The forecast chains mean crossing times along one path, but real fire tries every burning neighbour
  each tick. Over 200 simulated fires the 10th-percentile arrival was 0.44-0.59x the forecast. So "can I reach this
  person in time?" is judged against `save_margin` = 0.5x the forecast. The value was set from that measured error,
  not by tuning the benchmark.
* **Pickup order.** Every order of the people still waiting is tried (brute force over permutations; households are small), with travel
  time at the slowest follower's pace. Orders are ranked by a lexicographic priority key: (1) children reached before
  the pessimistic forecast, the most endangered child first; (2) no lower-priority person before a higher one
  (children and unknown hidden people, then adults, then the dog); (3) most people reached in time; (4) keep heading
  for the current target, so it doesn't flip-flop between equal plans; (5) whoever the fire reaches soonest; (6)
  cheapest route ending at an exit. A cell the belief marks `occupied` counts as a person waiting.
* **Exit in time.** When leading people out, it takes the cheapest exit the whole group can reach before the
  pessimistic forecast, at the slowest walker's pace (4/3 while carrying). If no exit is safe, it takes the cheapest.
* **Pacing and going back.** It waits for followers who fall behind when no fire is near (up to `max_wait` = 6 ticks),
  and turns back for a following child who has fallen behind with flames close.
* **Fire escape.** A rescuer standing in flames used to see every route as walled off and burn where it stood. Now it
  steps to the nearest cell at least 2 steps from any flame, or to an exit if that is as near (fire never enters an
  exit). Once burned it makes no new pickups and heads out with whoever it has.
* **Crossing to the exit.** If no fire-free route exists and everyone with the rescuer is being carried (nobody is
  walking behind), it crosses to the exit through the fewest burning cells, provided that is fewer than the burns it
  can still survive. Crossing as a general fallback was tried and rejected: walking children cannot follow through
  flames.

### 3.5 World mechanics (`world.py`, `dependents.py`, `blueprint.py`)

One tick: scheduled novelties take effect, the rescuer moves, dependents react, fire spreads and smoke diffuses, then
anyone in fire is lost (subject to burn tolerance) and anyone on an exit is rescued. Fire uses its own seeded random
stream, and door forcing and burn tolerance have streams of their own, so for a given seed every agent faces an
identical fire, whatever it does.

* **Households.** `single` (dog Rex), `married` (spouse Sam), `kids` (Ada and Max), `kids_dog`, `family` (spouse +
  2 kids), `family_dog` (spouse + 2 kids + dog).
* **Dependents.** The dog moves every tick, bolts away from fire within 2 walking steps (50% of the time), and
  follows with probability 0.70. Children move every other tick. The spouse acts on her own: she fetches the nearest
  known waiting child (who then follows her), or heads for the nearest exit, keeping 2 or more steps from flames when
  any route allows. Flames within 2 walking steps, or walking into a jammed door, start a 10-tick panic of random
  steps. Once the rescuer reaches her, she calms down and follows. The planner treats her as a pickup when she is
  panicking, close to flames, or has no believed route out.
* **Carrying.** When flames come within 3 walking steps of a child, the parent leading that child (the rescuer or
  the spouse) picks them up once beside them. They then share a cell (rescued or lost together), and the carrier
  skips every 4th tick (25% slower). Children only: carrying the fast dog slowed the rescuer near flames and cost
  lives. Followers leave by their leader's exit.
* **Burn tolerance.** The rescuer and the spouse each draw a tolerance of 2-5 ticks in flames (seeded) and fall when
  their total burns reach it. Children and the dog have none. A carried child is shielded and is lost only if the
  carrier falls.
* **Door forcing, from outside only.** Walking into a jammed door from its hallway side is also an attempt to kick
  it in (`force_prob:door`, 10% per try). From inside a room it can't be forced (doors swing inward). "Outside" is the
  side whose door-bounded area touches more doors (`Blueprint.door_outside`).
* **Hearing and reveal.** While the hidden visitor waits, every unused slot within Manhattan distance 4
  (`HEARING_RANGE`; sound carries through walls) logs an `occupied:<slot>` event: true if someone is there. From tick
  `REVEAL_STEP` = 10, a person still hiding is heard from anywhere. The visitor is absent from the observation until
  found, but counts in the score.
* **Smoke** diffuses and can shrink a visibility radius (`observe(full=False)`). The benchmark runs fully observed.

### 3.6 The reward (`reward.py`, `docs/REWARD.md`)

None of the planner agents learn from the reward. It is reported, audited, and used to train the learned agents
(section 6). Every step reports each component separately (`info["reward_components"]`):

| component | default | purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `time` | -0.02 per tick; charged to the 150-tick horizon on a timeout or partial ending | be quick; only full success stops the clock |
| `progress` | 0.03 x (potential(s') - potential(s)) | dense "getting closer" signal |
| `rescue` | +1.0 per dependent out | the point |
| `success` | +5.0 when everyone is out and the rescuer is at an exit | everyone out is worth far more than the parts |
| `fire_proximity` | -0.10 x closeness (0.75 / 0.5 / 0.25 at 1 / 2 / 3 walking steps) per known dependent per tick | stay clear of flames |
| `fire_contact` | -10.0 per dependent lost to flames, and per dependent still inside at a timeout | a lost life dwarfs everything |
| `agent_death` | everything carrying on would have cost, to the horizon, + 1.0 | dying must never be cheaper |

**Potential-based progress.** The potential is minus the steps each waiting or following dependent still needs to
reach an exit, minus the rescuer's distance to the nearest known waiting dependent, minus a large fixed amount for
each dependent lost. Distances are walked around walls and around only the jammed doors someone has bumped into
(`known_jams`), so the reward never reveals an unfound jam. Because progress is a difference of a function of the
state, it sums to `potential(end) - potential(start)` over an episode, and walking in circles earns nothing (Ng,
Harada and Russell, 1999). At the end of an episode every distance term is 0 and only a fixed charge per dependent
lost to the flames remains, so a failure earns no partial credit for where people stood or for a jam it never found
(fixed in the final verification, after `stall` and `door_shy` had out-scored an honest agent stuck at a jammed
door). A hidden person still waiting is left out of the dense terms, so the reward doesn't give away the hiding place.

**Audited against exploits.** Each known exploit has a test and a degenerate policy in `scripts/audit_reward.py`:
suicide, letting dependents burn, farming progress by looping, abandoning them, dawdling, reading the hiding place
or an unfound jammed door from the reward, letting them burn to stop the clock, leaving the endangered one behind,
and failing without trying the jammed door. An automated search (`scripts/find_exploits.py`) finds no confirmed
loophole in the current reward (960 episodes small budget, 4800 medium); against the legacy reward it once confirmed
the suicide loophole on fresh seeds, and with today's dynamics it finds it in-sample only (the hand-written suicide
policy still exposes it). A weight sweep (`scripts/reward_sweep.py`) shows that the "honest
first" and "dying never pays" checks hold at the defaults and break only at 4x the progress or proximity weight.

### 3.7 Evidence discipline and the lockstep leak test

The rule: only the oracle may read the true world. `make_agent` hands the world only to the oracle; every other agent
gets observations and events alone. The Gym encoder shows a jammed door only after the rescuer has bumped it, and the
env's mid-episode head count excludes hidden people. The proof is the **lockstep test**
(`tests/test_evidence_discipline.py`): a novelty world and a no-novelty world with the same seed are stepped side by
side. While the agent's inputs are identical in both, its decisions must be identical too, so nothing it did can have
come from the hidden truth. The oracle *fails* this test, which shows the test can catch a leak.
`tests/test_no_leaks.py` (37 tests) extends it to the `house`, more seeds, episode isolation (a run is identical
whether fresh or after hundreds of others), starting beliefs, and the hidden person's invisibility in observations,
encodings and dataset records.

### 3.8 Procedural houses (`procgen.py`)

`generate(seed, tier)` builds a room-and-corridor house (rooms joined by a random spanning tree of doorways, plus
extra links for loops) as ASCII, and parses it with the same `Blueprint.parse`. Difficulty is measured, not assumed:
`p_lost` (fire-only Monte Carlo chance the fire reaches a dependent before the earliest pickup) and `margin` (best
escape plan's safety margin against the forecast) set tiers 1-5, and the generator resamples until the measured tier
matches. Every house is checked for solvability, a jammable door and a spare slot for the hidden visitor. Use one with
`--blueprint gen:<tier>:<seed>`.

### 3.9 The trajectory dataset and validators (`dataset.py`, `docs/DATA.md`)

One JSON line per episode (schema v1): house and hash, household, novelty, seed, agent, per-step compact observation,
action, reason, reward components and events, plus outcome, return and efficiency. The hidden truth (which novelty,
which patches) lives only in episode-level `labels`. Builds are byte-identical for any worker count. Train/val/test
splits are by (house, seed) group. `scripts/validate_dataset.py` runs schema, **replay** (re-simulates every episode
and compares every step), leak, return = sum of components, outcome, duplicate, split-leakage, outlier and manifest
checks. The tests inject each kind of corruption and assert the right check fires. Oracle records are marked
`privileged`.

### 3.10 The RL environment (`env.py`)

`EmberPathEnv` is a Gymnasium environment (it needs the `rl` extra). Observation: a float32 `(H, W, 11)` grid with
channels for wall, fire, burnt, smoke, agent, waiting dependent, following dependent, exit, door, jammed door (only
once bumped) and time remaining ((150 - t) / 150, added so a policy can tell tick 3 from tick 90). Actions: up, down, left, right, wait. Reward: the audited reward above. Every ending is
`terminated` (the horizon is part of the reward), and `info` holds nothing the rescuer could not perceive until the
episode ends.

### 3.11 Evaluation (`evaluate.py`, `scripts/make_results.py`)

Score per episode = fraction of dependents rescued, or 0 if the rescuer dies. All agents play the same seeds, and so
the same fires. `gap_closed = (adaptive - baseline) / (oracle - baseline)` on mean score, reported as `n/a` when the
oracle gap is under 0.05. Intervals are 95% *paired* bootstraps over seeds (each resample takes the same seeds for
every agent, so the shared fire cancels out). `steps vs oracle` = adaptive steps / oracle steps, counted only on seeds
where both got everyone out, so dying early can never look fast. **The built-in control:** with no novelty, baseline
and oracle must be identical (oracle gap +0.00). Any non-zero there would be a harness bug.

## 5. Results

All numbers below are copied from the files named, as they stand now. Each table cell is 60 seeds (fires).
"Score" is the fraction of dependents rescued, 0 if the rescuer dies. "All out" is the share of fires where everyone
got out. The learned agents are evaluated separately, on 100 held-out fires (section 6): pooled over 8 ranch

### 5.4 Negative results, read honestly

* **`wood_flashover` produced no gap at all.** The first spread-rate novelty made wood burn faster, but in the ranch
  every exit route crosses the hallway, so knowing the true rate changed no decision. The lesson: a novelty only
  measures adaptation if a correct belief makes a *different, better* decision possible. (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`)
* **`carpet_flashover` no longer separates the agents on the ranch.** Survival-first pickup ordering already fetches
  the child the fire will reach first, which covers fast carpet too: baseline = oracle on ranch/kids. A strict `xfail`
  test (`test_carpet_flashover_has_an_oracle_gap_for_kids`) records this rather than deleting it. On the house the
  carpet novelty's gap is zero or negative.
* **The oracle is not an upper bound, and on `hidden_dependent` it is handicapped.** The planner treats an unknown
  person like a child, so it is fetched first. The oracle knows about the visitor from tick 0 and goes for a
  half-speed child before anyone else, which slows the whole rescue. The adaptive agent learns about the visitor later
  and usually collects the others first. Hence gap closed above 100% (ranch single 108%, house family 200%) and steps
  vs oracle below 1 (0.82). Told about the visitor at the start, the adaptive agent behaves exactly like the oracle.
  Whether the oracle should stop putting an unknown person first is an open decision.
* **Knowing sooner is not always better** (lifelong runs, `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`): carrying beliefs across episodes
  helped 3 of 12 cells and hurt 2 (single hidden_dependent 0.90 -> 0.75), for the same reason.
* **Ranch married dropped after wall-aware fire distances** (none: 0.97 -> 0.93; now 0.93 in `docs/results.md`).
  The old straight-line check through walls happened to make the rescuer turn back for the spouse. With correct
  geometry it leads out, and the fire later comes round through the doors and catches her. The correct geometry was
  kept; a forecast-based "the fire will reach her soon" check would be the honest fix.
* **Gap closed fell on some house rows after tile went to 15%**: family_dog door_jam 62% -> 44% (interval now
  includes 0), family door_jam 107% -> 77%, kids hidden_dependent 105% -> 78%. Tile went 0.02 -> 0.12 -> 0.25 -> 0.15;
  every change alters every fire.
* **House single compound (0.68) scores above house single none (0.52)** in `docs/results_house.md`. Two novelties
  together should not make a rescue easier. Nobody has explained this yet.
* **The repair search adds nothing on the shipped presets.** Each preset changes one key per flag, so the search
  returns exactly what the naive "jump to the posterior mean" ablation does. Its value (pairs, ambiguity) is only
  exercised in unit tests with confounded evidence.
* **Information-limited cells.** When the value of knowing a change disappears within a few ticks but the evidence
  takes longer to arrive, no detector can help: on single / compound an oracle told 3 ticks late drops from 0.57 to
  0.42, while the carpet evidence takes 6-9 ticks (the "oracle told k ticks late" curves in `docs/adaptive_study.md`).
* **Survival-first ordering has costs.** Going first for the endangered child can get the rescuer killed, and putting
  children before the dog lowers dog survival (79% -> 65% across the dog households) while raising children out
  (64% -> 73%).

## 6. Learned agents: PPO, BC+PPO and DreamerV3

Sources: [`docs/RL.md`](RL.md) (PPO, "Independent verification", "Improvements tried") and
[`docs/DREAMER.md`](DREAMER.md) (including "Planner-guided Dreamer"). Every number below is copied from those files and
was produced by the shared harness (`evaluate.run_episode`) on held-out seeds 1000..1099, the same 100 fires for every
agent; a sample was recomputed in the final verification and matched exactly.

### 6.1 In plain English

The planner agents of sections 2-3 cope with a changing world by keeping an explicit rulebook, noticing when the
evidence contradicts it, and repairing it mid-run. A learned agent has no rulebook at all. It is a neural network that
maps "what I see now" (the floor plan, the fire, where everyone is, the clock, any door it has bumped and found
jammed) straight to a move, and it gets good at that by playing thousands of practice fires and being scored by the
audited reward. The changes (jammed doors, faster carpet, a hidden visitor) happen in its practice fires too, so the
hope is that it learns habits that cope with them, such as "after bumping a stuck door, take the long way round" or
"if I hear someone calling, go and look". It never says *which* rule changed; it only acts.

Three were tried:

* **PPO from scratch** (a standard deep-RL algorithm). After 30 CPU minutes of practice it got everyone out of 42% of
  fires with the dog and 7% with two children, where the planner gets 89% and 96%. It never learned the jammed door or
  the hidden visitor. **Far below the planner.**
* **BC+PPO.** First it copies the adaptive planner's moves on practice fires (behaviour cloning, "BC"), then PPO
  practice fine-tunes it on the reward. It also hears calls (an extra input showing where it last heard someone) and
  is never offered a move into a wall. **It matches the adaptive planner in every setting it practised and edges past
  it overall** (0.93 against 0.90 on the 8 practised ranch settings), most clearly with the dog and a hidden visitor.
  But it learned from the planner, it was trained once, and it does worse than the planner on a combination of
  changes it never practised.
* **DreamerV3** (learns a model of the world and practises inside its own imagination). **It learned nothing usable**
  in 82 CPU minutes: 0% everywhere, even when its practice data was seeded with the planner's rescues. It was tried
  and abandoned.

So the two families answer the same problem differently: the planner adapts by detecting and repairing beliefs
during the rescue; the learned agents learn, in advance, a policy that copes with the changes they were trained on.

### 6.2 Technically

* **Environment and input.** `EmberPathEnv` (section 3.10): `env.encode` gives 11 planes (wall, fire, burnt, smoke,
  agent, waiting dependent, following dependent, exit, door, bumped jam, time remaining). The policies drop smoke
  (an `Observation` carries smoke only at the rescuer's cell, so it could not be rebuilt at test time), pad smaller
  houses to the 13x24 ranch canvas, and choose from 5 actions. Reward: the audited reward of section 3.6, scaled by 0.1
  for the learning targets only. Every ending is a true termination.
* **Evidence discipline.** `PPOAgent` and `DreamerAgent` rebuild their input from the `Observation` and their own
  `Event`s (a jam appears only after their own bump; the hearing plane only from their own `occupied:<slot>` events),
  never from the world. Tests check the rebuilt input equals what training saw, over whole episodes.
* **Seeds.** Training fires come from seeds 0..999 only, evaluation from 1000..1099; disjointness is asserted in the
  module, at the end of training and in `evaluate_policy`. Intervals are 95% paired bootstraps over the 100 evaluation
  fires (2,000 resamples).
* **PPO** (`rl_baseline.py`): conv 3x3 (16) -> conv 3x3 stride 2 (32) -> 256 -> policy and value heads; GAE (0.99,
  0.95), clip 0.2, entropy 0.01, Adam 3e-4 annealed, 32 envs x 128 steps per update. Curriculum: studio warm-up 150k
  steps, ranch 1.0 M, then ranch with the novelties sampled per episode for 1.0 M (2.2 M steps, 1776 s on 8 cores).
* **BC+PPO** (`collect_teacher`, `train_bc`, flags on `scripts/train_ppo.py`): the teacher is the evidence-disciplined
  adaptive agent (the oracle is refused), run through the gym env on training seeds only: 3,600 episodes, 226,532
  states over 9 settings (ranch dog / kids x 4 novelty settings, plus studio / dog). Cross-entropy plus 0.5 x value
  MSE, 6 epochs. Policy-side additions: the hearing plane (input 11; `env.encode` itself is unchanged) and invalid-move
  masking from the wall and agent planes only (doors are never masked, since a jammed door can be kicked in). Then
  600k PPO steps from the clone: 10 value-only updates, a KL pull toward the clone (0.5, fading to 0 by half the run),
  lr 1e-4, studio mixed in against forgetting. Wall clock 489 s BC + 811 s PPO on 4 shared cores.
* **DreamerV3** (`dreamer.py`): RSSM (GRU 256 + 16x16 categorical latents), the same CNN encoder with a mirrored
  decoder, two-hot symlog reward and value heads, continue head, imagined actor-critic. 273k env steps (31k studio +
  242k ranch), about 4,600 updates, 82 CPU minutes. Planner-guided variant: the replay is seeded with 82k transitions of
  the adaptive agent on training seeds (1,200 ranch episodes), 50% of each batch tapered to exactly 0 by 60% of the
  run; acting is always autonomous.

### 6.4 Caveats, read before quoting the numbers

* **One training seed.** Every learned result is one training run. The intervals cover evaluation fires only, not
  training-seed variance, which for PPO is often larger.
* **About 33 comparisons against the planners.** One or two intervals excluding 0 by chance would not be surprising,
  so the marginal wins (kids / hidden_dependent, dog / carpet_flashover, lower bounds at 0.00) may be chance. The
  dog / hidden_dependent win (+0.09 [+0.05, +0.14] over the oracle, rising at every checkpoint) is well clear of that.
* **Held-out means new fires in the same houses.** The houses, the door that jams, the hiding places and the carpet
  room are the same in training and evaluation. A learned policy can absorb those regularities (for example where the
  visitor usually hides once heard); the planner does not use them. That is legitimate learning from the training
  distribution, but it is not evidence of generalisation to new houses. The one unseen novelty, `compound`, is where
  BC+PPO falls behind the planner.
* **BC+PPO is imitation plus RL, not RL from scratch.** It starts as a copy of the adaptive planner, which already
  solves the jammed door and the hidden visitor; the clone alone reaches 0.894. Only the +0.036 from fine-tuning is
  the RL contribution. RL from scratch is the 0.327 row.
* **Part of the hidden-visitor win is the oracle's known handicap.** The planner treats an unknown person like a
  child and goes for them first (section 5.4), which slows the whole rescue; the adaptive agent inherits the same
  priority once it hears the call. A learned policy is free to collect the others first, so some of its edge on
  `hidden_dependent` is that handicap rather than better rescuing.
* **The checkpoints predate the final reward fix.** All learned agents were trained before the end-of-episode
  potential fix of section 3.6 (and the original PPO before the jam-leak fix); they were not retrained. Their scores
  do not depend on the reward at evaluation time, but a retrained policy could differ.

### 6.5 DreamerV3: tried and abandoned (a negative result), and why

Dreamer is meant to be sample-efficient, but on this CPU budget (82 minutes, about 4,600 updates, one to two orders of
magnitude below the paper's runs) its policy never leaves the starting room: 0% held-out success in every setting,
below PPO from scratch (paired score differences -0.25 to -0.46 in 8 of 10 settings) and far below the planner. It
also failed the studio sanity check (random-level 1% at the end). The diagnostics say why: the world model learned the
static house and the small per-tick costs, but not where the rescuer is (decoded in the right cell 42-58% of frames)
or the large rewards at the end of an episode (it predicts about -0.2 where the real charge is -3 to -19, and the
critic gives the same value, -6.21, to every start). Seeding the replay with the adaptive planner's rescues did not
help (0% everywhere, at the moment the demo share hit zero and at the end): even trained on nothing but planner
demos, its reward head predicts the same +0.06 on success steps (real +6.0), loss steps (real -6.7) and ordinary
steps, while the same encoder and reward head *without* the recurrent latent learn the success reward from the same
data in 400 updates. **The reward signal is lost in the RSSM latent; the bottleneck is the representation, not a lack
of rescues in the data.** This is a budget result, not proof that a world model cannot help here. The Dreamer work
was then stopped: the code and its tests (`tests/test_dreamer.py`) are kept and pass, but no further Dreamer training
or evaluation was done.

## 8. Development history

The project began as a scaffold: world, fire, households, the symbolic planner, baseline and oracle agents, the
audited reward and a Gym wrapper, with the detector, repair and adaptive agent stubbed out as failing acceptance tests.
The rest was built over three days, mostly by parallel coding agents with a tracker and an independent final checker.
The major decisions, in order:

**Day 1: the adaptation loop.**
* **A shared contract first.** The `occupied:<r>,<c>` rule was added to the rule grammar before any parallel work
  started, so the detector, repair and new-novelty work all built against one interface.
* **The hidden dependent novelty**: an unexpected person, semi-structural but expressed as a boolean rule, heard
  rather than seen.
* **Detector, repair, adaptive agent.** The detector treats every yes/no rule the same way (no door-specific code).
  The repair search reports a runner-up and ambiguity. The first adaptive agent closed 97% / 98% / 65% of the
  door-jam gap for single / married / kids against a 50% target, with no false alarms in 300 quiet episodes.
* **Track B in parallel:** procedural houses with measured tiers, the trajectory dataset with replay validators, and
  automated reward-exploit search, which rediscovered the old "dying scores better" loophole.
* **A trim pass** made evaluation about 1.9x faster, with every benchmark episode byte-identical before and after.

**Day 2: making the rescue realistic and the benchmark honest.**
* **Evidence discipline.** Prompted by "can the agent see anything the oracle sees?": only the oracle now receives the
  world, the encoder hides unbumped jams, and the lockstep test proves it. `emberpath improve` added first and last
  GIFs for lifelong runs.
* **Reveal at tick 10.** With hearing alone, about 23% of fires never brought the rescuer within earshot, capping gap
  closed near 77%. Widening the range would make the novelty trivial, so a late, fixed-time reveal was added instead.
* **Heavy penalty for losing a child, and survival-first ordering.** After GIFs showed children burning, the loss
  penalty was raised to -10 (with success +5 and timeouts charged like losses). The key point raised at the time:
  the planner isn't trained on the reward, so the fix that changed behaviour was in the planner. It now fetches
  whoever the fire reaches first, judged against a pessimistic forecast (`save_margin`). Both children got out in
  92% of quiet ranch fires, up from 67%.
* **A spouse who acts alone, with panic, and the family household.** **Door forcing** was first allowed from both
  sides, which erased the door-jam gap at any rate (the baseline's stubborn bumping became forcing). Restricting it
  to outside-only restored the gap exactly.
* **A wider house** (`blueprints/house.txt`), which the user then redrew. The benchmark now cycles all six households
  through every novelty on any blueprint.
* **Followers leave by their leader's exit** (a bug: the spouse's followers switched to the rescuer once she was out).
  **Carrying** at 25% slower, then children only after carrying the dog cost lives. **Exit in time.** The carpet
  novelty was tried at 60%, 40% and 50%, and settled at 45%.
* **Kids before the dog, the most endangered child first**, after a GIF showed the rescuer leaving Max to burn while
  fetching Rex. Also: stick with the current target (no Max/Rex flip-flop), and trigger carrying by flames near the
  *child* (walking distance, not through walls).
* **Burn tolerance of 2-5 ticks** made turning back for a lagging child worth it.
* **Fire escape**, after a rescuer burned standing still because every route looked walled off. Crossing flames as a
  general fallback was tried and rejected. Then **exits count as safe places**, and crossing a little fire is allowed
  when everyone with the rescuer is carried.
* **Steps vs oracle**, added to answer "how close is the adaptive agent to the oracle's path?", because the existing
  efficiency metric was not comparable between agents.
* **Leak audits, tile back to 15%, wall-aware distances.** A second audit found no novelty leak and no memory between
  episodes (37 new tests). Tile spread settled at 15% after 2%, 12% and 25%. Five checks that measured distance to fire
  in straight lines through walls (spouse panic, keeping clear of flames, the dog fleeing, waiting for stragglers) or
  routed fire through burnt cells were made wall-aware. An RL check found and fixed three env and dataset bugs.

**Day 3: learned agents, verification and this write-up.**
* **PPO from scratch** with a time-remaining channel (a greedy policy otherwise looped, unable to tell tick 3 from
  tick 90): 2.2 M steps, far below the planner, never learned the jammed door or the hidden visitor.
* **An independent check** of PPO's maths (13 tests; one NaN bug for one-sample minibatches fixed), of the reward
  (the progress potential leaked an unfound jammed door: `jam_snoop` turned it into oracle-level play from its own
  payments; fixed with `known_jams`), and of evidence discipline on the real checkpoint (lockstep test, 0 differing
  decisions).
* **BC+PPO**: cloning the adaptive agent on training fires, plus a hearing plane and wall mask, then PPO fine-tuning,
  reached 0.93 pooled against 0.90 for its teacher. A recurrent (GRU) clone did slightly worse.
* **DreamerV3** reached 0% everywhere, unguided and with planner-seeded replay; the probe traced it to the reward
  signal being lost in the RSSM latent. The Dreamer work was stopped.
* **Final verification**: every quickstart command run, the benchmark regenerated byte-identical, a sample of the RL
  numbers recomputed, and one reward issue fixed: a failed episode kept its final potential as partial credit, which
  let `stall` and `door_shy` out-score an honest agent stuck at a jammed door (an invariant-8 violation). The distance
  terms are now 0 at every terminal state.

## 9. Limitations and future work

**Limitations**

* **A 2D toy world.** Fire is a cellular automaton, not physics; one floor; no real sensors. The claim is about the
  adaptation loop and the environment engineering, not fire fidelity.
* **Parameter novelties only.** Every change is a new value for an existing rule key. Genuinely structural novelty (a
  new kind of object, a new action such as exiting through a window) is out of scope. The hidden visitor is a new
  person, but still a rule key.
* **The repair search is untested on hard cases.** No shipped preset needs more than "jump to the posterior mean".
* **The oracle is a yardstick, not an optimum.** It is the same cautious planner given the truth, and it sometimes
  loses to the adaptive agent. Gap closed above 100% means that, not super-oracle skill.
* **The planner is not risk-aware enough to use early knowledge well** (lifelong runs and the hidden-dependent
  oracle).
* **Several benchmark cells measure survival, not adaptation**: ranch carpet_flashover everywhere, house kids and
  kids_dog, and cells with low ceilings such as compound.
* **One house, one number.** `save_margin`, carpet 45% and tile 15% were each chosen on these two houses. Gap closed on
  house rows moved by up to 30 points when tile changed, so read single cells with their intervals.
* **The full "what changed, how sure, what I did" report is not built.** `explain_adaptation` still raises
  `NotImplementedError`. The agent explains itself through per-move reasons and belief-change announcements; the GIF
  banner shows when the truth changed but not yet when the agent noticed.
* **The learned agents are one training run each, on two fixed houses.** BC+PPO's edge over the planner (+0.026
  pooled) is on new fires in the same houses, includes the oracle's "unknown person first" handicap, and comes from
  imitating the planner first; on the unseen `compound` novelty it is below the planner. DreamerV3 learned nothing.
* **The learned checkpoints predate the final reward fix** (terminal potential) and were not retrained.
* **Discounted learners still see a small shaping residual**: PPO discounts with gamma 0.99 while the shaping uses
  gamma 1, which leaves "never try the jammed door" up to 0.4 ahead of "stuck at it" in the *discounted* return
  (undiscounted, they tie; `docs/REWARD.md`).
* **Fully observed benchmark.** The smoke-limited view exists but no belief tracker uses it, and the dataset records
  full observations.
* **Unexplained oddities** remain (house single compound > none).

**Future work**

* Learned agents: several training seeds, held-out houses (procedural ones), DAgger, PPO from scratch with the
  hearing plane and mask (to separate their effect from cloning), and retraining under the current reward.
* `explain_adaptation` and a "noticed at t=..." GIF banner.
* A fairer oracle (don't put an unknown person first), and a forecast-based "the fire will reach them soon" check in
  place of the lost straight-line proxy.
* Shaping consistent with the learner's discount (gamma x potential(s') - potential(s)).
* Presets with confounded or multi-key evidence, so the repair search earns its keep.
* Change-point inference (when did it change; forget stale evidence), structural novelty, triage when not everyone can
  be saved, a CNN perception front-end, an LLM-agent interface, a post-training data study, and a planner speed-up
  with identical results (`ROADMAP.md`, `tasks/`).

## 10. Glossary

| term | meaning |
|---|---|
| **agent / rescuer** | the program that decides where to move each tick |
| **dependent** | someone the rescuer must get out: dog, spouse, child, or a hidden visitor |
| **household** | which dependents are in the house (single, married, kids, kids_dog, family, family_dog) |
| **tick** | one turn of the clock; everyone moves once, then the fire spreads |
| **seed** | a number that fixes the randomness; same seed, same fire for every agent |
| **rulebook / rules** | the probabilities that govern the world (how fast things burn, which doors open) |
| **belief** | the agent's version of the rulebook, which may be wrong |
| **novelty** | a secret change to the true rules partway through a rescue |
| **baseline** | the agent that never updates its rulebook |
| **oracle** | the agent that is always told the true rules; a reference, not a fair competitor |
| **adaptive agent** | the agent that has to work out the change from evidence |
| **gap closed** | how much of the baseline-to-oracle difference the adaptive agent recovers (100% = matches the oracle) |
| **control row** | the "no novelty" case, where baseline and oracle must tie; a check on the harness |
| **Bernoulli / coin** | a yes/no event with a fixed chance, like a biased coin |
| **posterior** | the updated estimate of a probability after seeing evidence |
| **Bayes factor** | how many times better one explanation fits the data than another |
| **SPRT** | sequential probability ratio test: a test that can be checked after every observation without inflating false alarms |
| **false alarm** | flagging a change when nothing changed |
| **repair / RulePatch** | a single edit to a rule; a novelty patches the truth, a repair patches the belief |
| **runner-up / ambiguous** | the second-best explanation; "ambiguous" when it fits almost as well as the best |
| **Dijkstra** | a standard algorithm for the cheapest route through a weighted grid |
| **fire ETA** | forecast time for the fire to reach each cell |
| **save margin** | the safety factor (0.5) applied to the fire forecast when deciding if someone can be reached in time |
| **reward** | the score signal a learning agent is trained on; here also audited for loopholes |
| **potential-based shaping** | a "getting closer" bonus built so that it can't be farmed and can't change the best behaviour |
| **reward hacking / exploit** | a way to score well without doing the task (for example, dying early to stop a time penalty) |
| **evidence discipline** | the rule that only the oracle may see the true world |
| **lockstep test** | running a changed and an unchanged world side by side to prove the agent can't see the difference until evidence arrives |
| **procedural generation / tier** | building houses by algorithm; tier = measured difficulty 1-5 |
| **paired bootstrap interval** | a 95% range for a result, computed by resampling the same fires for every agent |
| **steps vs oracle** | adaptive steps divided by oracle steps, on fires both fully succeeded |
| **PPO** | proximal policy optimization, a standard deep reinforcement-learning algorithm |
| **policy** | a learned agent's mapping from what it sees to a move, with no explicit rulebook |
| **behaviour cloning (BC)** | training a network to copy a teacher's moves; here the teacher is the adaptive planner |
| **BC+PPO** | a BC clone fine-tuned with PPO on the reward |
| **DreamerV3 / world model / RSSM** | an RL method that learns a model of the world (a recurrent state-space model) and trains its policy inside the model's imagination |
| **Gymnasium env** | the standard interface RL libraries use to talk to a simulator |
| **xfail** | a test marked "expected to fail", used here to record a known negative result |
